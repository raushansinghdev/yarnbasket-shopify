Decided · base theme

We start from Shopify's Skeleton theme. It's Shopify's official minimal starter (github.com/Shopify/skeleton-theme), with the Online Store 2.0 structure and theme blocks and nothing else. We write every section ourselves and never spend days stripping out someone else's code. For the hard, well-tested parts (the accessible cart drawer, predictive search, filters, the variant picker) we read how Dawn and Horizon do it, then write our own version.

Research

What the reference stores teach us

I read each home page's structure from its HTML. Before we design the home page we'll also walk through all five on a phone.

Floreal

floreal.in · crochet bouquets

TakeOccasion gift boxes (Rakhi, Mother's Day), a founder's note written in a human voice, and "view all" links that keep the product rows short.

AvoidAbout 9 product rows and 12 category tiles on one page, a Shark Tank badge repeated many times, duplicate menus, and "43% OFF" badges on almost everything.

Knots n Gifts

knotsngifts.in · crochet gifts

TakeCollections by occasion and by who it's for (couple gifts), a clear "ships in 5–7 days" promise, press mentions, and an artisan mission story.

AvoidSeveral promo bars stacked at the top and a modal that pops up on page load.

Rare You

rareyou.com · handmade decor

TakeThe calmest structure of the group. A short "why us" row (handcrafted, women-led, add a note, quality yarn), an FAQ that explains partial COD, and one big trust number.

AvoidGrids of 11 to 20 products on the home page, and a wall of "sold out" labels.

Raask Crochet

raaskcrochet.com · crochet bouquets

TakeA cream palette, generous spacing and a short home page of about 8 sections. This is the closest to our direction.

AvoidEmoji in product titles, which hurts search and screen readers. Also a rotating hero carousel and 20+ bestsellers on one page.

Deep Suhani

deepsuhani.in · herbal hair care

TakeTrust built through transparency: a "how it's made" infographic, a founder photo, ratings shown with review counts, and reviews signed with a name and city in Hindi and English.

AvoidA very long page, testimonials repeated twice, and an expired offer still on show.

The gap we can own: every crochet store here competes on discounts and quantity. Yarn Basket can win by being the calm one: fewer, better-photographed products, plenty of space, and a clear story of who makes each piece and how long it takes. Gifting is central to the whole category, so the gift experience has to be excellent: shopping by occasion, a gift note, and wrapping.

From the brand kit

Brand direction

The Instagram grid in the brand kit already has a complete visual system. The store reuses it, so the shop, the Instagram profile, the coming-soon page and the parcels all feel like one brand.

Three surfaces in a rhythm

The grid alternates Blush cards, Cocoa cards and Oat photo frames. The home page does the same: mostly Oat, one Blush band and one Cocoa band. Sections separate through colour, with no dividers adding clutter.

Stitch borders

The covers' dashed inner border looks like a row of stitches (dashes 14 / gaps 12, rounded caps, Cocoa at 32%). It frames feature panels and the promise strip, and it can sew itself in as it scrolls into view.

Photo frame as product card

An Oat card, a rounded photo window, a Cormorant italic caption and a tiny tracked label. That is our product card, so products look the same on Instagram and on the site.

Cover layout as section heading

Line icon, then a tracked eyebrow, a two-line serif headline and one plain line of text. Every section heading follows this order.

Line icons already drawn

Bouquet, keychain heart, hair clip, reviews, basket, bag charm, yarn ball and hook, care basin. We export them as one SVG sprite and draw the missing UI icons in the same 2px style.

The yarn strand

The pinned banner runs one yarn strand from the ball across all three tiles to a flower. That becomes the store's signature scroll animation.

Voice: the cover lines become section headings: "Flowers that never fade", "Tiny, cute & all yours", "One stitch at a time", "Little touches of handmade", "Your words make our day". The full mapping is saved in docs/brand-direction.md.

How it should feel

Six principles

Space is a feature

Every section gets one idea, one heading and at most one main button. If a section needs a second idea, it becomes a second section or a separate page.

Thumb first

Design at 360px wide first. Anything important sits within thumb reach, every tap target is at least 48px, and the cart is always one tap away.

Alive, not busy

Motion responds to what the shopper does: a press, a scroll, an add to cart. Nothing moves on a loop to grab attention, and nothing rotates by itself.

Fast is premium

A beautiful page that loads in 5 seconds on a Redmi over 4G is not premium. Speed budgets are hard limits, not targets.

Honest commerce

Real reviews with names and cities, a delivery promise we can keep, and quiet sale prices with no shouting badges.

Search engines read it too

Every page has one clear H1, real text, structured data and descriptive image alt text. SEO is built into each section, not added at the end.

Design system

5 – 18 Oct

Turn the brand kit into tokens and components that every section reuses. Lock this before the home page and nothing will drift later.

Tokens

  • Colour roles with contrast checked, taken from the brand kit (see Brand direction and Design system).
  • A fluid type scale using clamp(), from 360px up to 1440px.
  • A spacing scale and section rhythm, the core defence against clutter.
  • Motion tokens for duration and easing (see Motion).
  • Fonts served from our own store (Shopify's font library or files in assets/), not from Google, to save a connection on every page.

Components

  • Button, text link, icon button, badge, price, star rating.
  • Product card, the most reused piece. It shows a second image on hover or long-press.
  • Accordion, drawer, toast, chip, input, quantity stepper.
  • Skeleton placeholders for loading states. No spinners.
  • The brand kit's line icons exported as an SVG sprite, with any missing ones (cart, search, menu, account) drawn in the same style.
  • The stitch border and the photo-frame card from the Instagram covers.
  • A living style guide at a hidden page (/pages/styleguide) that shows every component.

Done when the style guide page shows every token and component correctly on a real phone, and each component passes keyboard and screen reader checks.

Audit pass

16 – 29 Nov

Every page was checked as it was built. This phase checks the whole site end to end, the way a shopper and Google will see it.

  • SEO: crawl the whole store (Screaming Frog is free for up to 500 URLs). Check titles, descriptions, canonical tags, alt text, broken links and orphan products. Run every template through Google's Rich Results Test.

Phase 1 detail

Design system

Colour contrast check

I measured each brand colour against our backgrounds using the WCAG 2.2 formula. Normal text needs 4.5:1. Large text (24px and up) and icons need 3:1.

ColourOn OatOn CreamOn BlushUse it for
Cocoa6.50 ✓6.91 ✓5.33 ✓All text, buttons, focus rings. Cream text on a Cocoa button is 6.91 ✓
Taupe3.79 ✗4.03 ✗3.11 ✗Large headings and decoration only. Fails for small text.
Taupe Ink (new)5.56 ✓5.91 ✓4.56 ✓Taupe darkened to pass. For captions, labels and secondary text.
Rose1.80 ✗1.91 ✗1.48 ✗Fills, underlines and illustration only. Never text, and never the only focus ring.
Sage1.83 ✗1.94 ✗1.50 ✗Backgrounds of sections and badges, with Cocoa text on top (3.56, large text only).
Fix carried over from the coming-soon page

The coming-soon button uses a Rose focus ring, which is only 1.8:1 against Oat. In the store, the focus ring will be a 2px Cocoa line with a soft Rose halo outside it, which passes and still looks on-brand.

Type scale (mobile → desktop)

  • Display: Cormorant Garamond Italic 600, 40 → 72px
  • H2: 32 → 48px · H3: 24 → 30px
  • Body: Jost 400, 16 → 17px, line height 1.6, at most 65 characters per line
  • Labels: Jost 500 uppercase, 12–13px, letter spacing 0.16–0.28em
  • Prices: Jost 500 with tabular figures so digits line up

Space (the anti-clutter system)

  • Base unit 4px. Scale: 4 · 8 · 12 · 16 · 24 · 32 · 48 · 64 · 96 · 128
  • Between sections: 72px on mobile, 128px on desktop
  • Side gutter: 20px on mobile, 40px on tablet, 64px on desktop. Content is at most 1280px wide.
  • Product grid gaps: 16px horizontal and 32px vertical on mobile, 32px and 56px on desktop
  • Heading to content: always 24–32px. Never squeezed.

Making it feel alive

Motion system

The site feels alive when it answers every touch and pages flow into each other, not when things move everywhere. Almost all of it runs on modern CSS that Chrome on Android (most of our shoppers) supports today, and it falls back gracefully on other browsers.

TokenValueUsed for
--dur-press120msButton press, tap feedback
--dur-state200msHover, colour changes, swatch selection
--dur-panel320msDrawers, accordions, bottom sheets
--dur-enter560msContent arriving on scroll
--dur-hero900msThe single orchestrated moment on each page
--ease-outcubic-bezier(.22, 1, .36, 1)Most motion (already used on the coming-soon page)
--ease-springlinear(…) spring curveCart bounce, drawer settle

Page transitions

Cross-document View Transitions (@view-transition { navigation: auto }). The product image morphs from the card into the product page, and the header stays put. This needs no single-page app.

Instant pages

Speculation Rules prerender a page when a finger touches its link, so the next page is often ready before the tap ends.

Scroll

Scroll-driven CSS animations (animation-timeline: view()) for content entering the screen and for the yarn thread. Browsers without them get a small IntersectionObserver fallback. Native scrolling always, never smooth-scroll libraries that hijack it.

Add to cart

The button turns into a tick, a small product image flies to the cart icon, the count bounces and the drawer opens. All of it happens at once (optimistic), then syncs with Shopify.

Panels

Drawers, menus and sheets use @starting-style and the native popover and dialog elements, so they animate without a library and come with keyboard and focus handling built in.

Hard rules

Animate only transform, opacity and clip-path. Content is visible without JavaScript. With reduced motion, everything becomes a short fade. It must hold 60fps on a mid-range Android phone. No GSAP unless a moment truly needs it.

Target: WCAG 2.2 AA

Accessibility

Built in from the start

  • Real HTML first: button, a, dialog, details, form labels. ARIA only where HTML falls short.
  • One H1 per page, headings in order, and landmarks (header, nav, main, footer).
  • Tap targets of at least 48px. WCAG requires 24px, and we go further.
  • A visible focus ring everywhere, never hidden behind the sticky header.
  • Carousels have buttons, never move by themselves, and work by swipe or keyboard.
  • Cart and form updates are announced to screen readers.
  • Pages still work at 200% zoom and at 320px wide.
  • lang="en-IN", and Hindi words marked lang="hi" where we use them.

Content rules

  • Alt text describes the piece: the colour, the flower and the size ("Pink and white crochet rose bouquet, 12 stems, wrapped in kraft paper").
  • Colour is never the only signal. Sold-out states also have a word and a strike-through.
  • Prices are read in full: "₹1,299, was ₹1,599".
  • Error messages say what's wrong and how to fix it.

Testing

  • axe in CI on every pull request, and the axe DevTools extension while building.
  • A keyboard-only purchase every week.
  • VoiceOver on iPhone and TalkBack on Android before each phase closes.

The top priority

SEO

Keywords and structure

  • Group searches into clusters. By product: crochet bouquet, crochet flowers, crochet keychain, crochet hair clips, crochet bag charm. By occasion: anniversary, birthday, Valentine's. By modifier: handmade, everlasting, online India, under ₹999.
  • Each cluster gets one page: a product type collection or an occasion collection. Blog posts link to it.
  • Clean URLs: /collections/crochet-bouquets, /products/pink-rose-crochet-bouquet.
  • Product links always go to /products/…, never /collections/x/products/…, so there is one URL per product.
  • Rename image files to describe the piece before uploading.

Structured data (JSON-LD)

  • Home: Organization (logo, Instagram, Meesho) and WebSite with site search.
  • Product: Offer with price and stock, shipping details, return policy, and AggregateRating from real reviews.
  • BreadcrumbList on collections and products, Article on blog posts.
  • FAQPage on FAQs. Google now shows FAQ rich results only for government and health sites, so this mainly helps AI answer engines understand us.
  • Every template checked in Google's Rich Results Test.

Content

  • 8–10 cornerstone posts before launch, for example: "How to care for crochet flowers", "Crochet bouquet vs real flowers", "Anniversary gifts under ₹999", "What is a bag charm and how to style it".
  • Seasonal guides published 4–6 weeks before Valentine's, Rakhi, Diwali and Christmas.
  • Every post links to at least 2 collections and 3 products.

Technical and launch

  • While the store is password-protected, Google can't see it. Indexing starts on launch day, and that's expected.
  • Launch day: verify Search Console, submit the sitemap, request indexing of the home page and top collections.
  • Google Merchant Center free listings through the Google & YouTube app, to show up in Shopping results.
  • llms.txt for AI crawlers: Shopify can't host files at the domain root directly, so we'll check during Phase 7 whether a URL redirect to a page works.
  • Tune robots.txt.liquid so search engines skip filter and sort URLs.

Definition of done

A section only counts as finished when all of these are true. This is how SEO, accessibility, motion and speed get built in rather than bolted on at the end.

  • Looks right at 360, 390, 768, 1024 and 1440px, with nothing cramped or overlapping.
  • Follows the spacing scale, with one idea and at most one main button.
  • Editable in the theme editor, with sensible defaults and limited settings.
  • Works with the keyboard alone, with visible focus and correct screen reader output.
  • Text and icons pass the contrast check.
  • Motion uses the tokens, runs at 60fps on the Android test phone, and becomes a fade with reduced motion.
  • Content is visible and usable without JavaScript.
  • Correct heading level, real text, alt text, and valid structured data where relevant.
  • Within the speed budget. Lighthouse mobile shows no new LCP, CLS or INP regressions.
  • theme check, axe and Lighthouse CI all pass on the pull request.
